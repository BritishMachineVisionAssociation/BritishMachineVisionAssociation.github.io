## Adrian Hilton

![Adrian Hilton](2024-hilton.jpg "Adrian Hilton")

Every year the BMVA bestows the title of Distinguished Fellow of the BMVA on an individual in recognition of their services to the British machine vision community. Tonight it is my great privilege to introduce the awardee for 2025, Professor Adrian Hilton.

Adrian Hilton is Professor of Computer Vision and AI at the University of Surrey. His name is synonymous with the acclaimed Centre for Vision, Speech and Signal Processing (or CVSSP), which he has led as director for nearly fifteen years, and since 2021 he has been the founding director of the Surrey Institute for People-Centred AI. CVSSP is one of the largest UK research groups in audio-visual machine perception, with 180 researchers, and the centre has a forty-year history of outstanding research across computer vision, machine learning, spatial audio, 3D/4D video, medical image analysis and multimedia communication systems, as well as an extensive track record of industry collaboration and technology transfer.

Adrian earned his BSc in Mechanical Engineering and then a DPhil in Stochastic Signal Processing from the University of Sussex in 1991. Since joining the University of Surrey, he has led research on 3D and pioneered 4D computer vision – a field I know well – including shape and scene reconstruction, as well as spatial audio and, more broadly, machine learning, graphics and perception. I guess we would call this perceptual AI these days! In addition to his academic contributions, Adrian has a strong record in the difficult task of technology transfer, with many successful industrial partnerships and collaborations across the creative and entertainment industries, as well as in manufacturing and healthcare. A notable example is his Royal Society Industry Fellowship with Framestore on the use of multi-view 3D video for film production. The creative industries have always been of special interest to Adrian, and he was one of the founding members of the European Conference on Visual Media Production (CVMP), which has now been running for 23 years.

On a personal note, beyond his research and innovation leadership, I know Adrian to be a shining advocate for the training and development of the next generation of researchers. He has championed the role of universities in this respect and has also shown great kindness in supporting and mentoring early-career researchers – not just his own students (who have gone on to successful roles across academia and industry) but others across the computer vision community too. I know I speak for many when I thank him for the generosity he has shown.

It comes as no surprise that Adrian has received much recognition for all his endeavours. He is a Fellow of the Institution of Engineering and Technology and a recipient of the IET Achievement Medal, as well as a Fellow of the International Association for Pattern Recognition. His research has received two EU IST Prizes, a DTI Award, a Computer Graphics World Innovation Award and eight best paper awards. He was a recipient of a coveted Royal Society Wolfson Research Merit Award and was made a Fellow of the Royal Academy of Engineering in 2019.

Adrian continues to lead cutting-edge vision research in the UK, most recently through Surrey's role as the core research partner for the AHRC CoSTAR National Laboratory for Creative Industry R&D, where he heads its technical research on Creative AI.

In summary, I think we can all agree that this recognition is long overdue. So, without further ado, I give you the 2025 BMVA Distinguished Fellow, Adrian Hilton!

Neill Campbell (BMVA Chair)
